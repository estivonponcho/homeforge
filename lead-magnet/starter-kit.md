## The core idea

Buy **local-first** and **things you own**. Cloud gadgets get bricked when a
company folds or paywalls a feature. The gear below keeps working when the
internet — or the vendor — doesn't.

---

## If you buy nothing else, buy these three first

1. **A Home Assistant hub** — the brain. Start with the plug-and-play option;
   don't over-build on day one.
2. **A Zigbee/Thread radio** — a small USB stick so your sensors don't depend on
   any one vendor's cloud.
3. **One good multipack of contact + motion sensors** — enough to automate one
   room and get hooked.

That's a complete, useful smart home for well under the cost of a single
"premium" cloud camera subscription over two years.

---

## The 5 mistakes that cost beginners the most

1. **Buying Wi-Fi everything.** Wi-Fi smart devices flood your network and often
   need the vendor cloud. Prefer Zigbee/Thread/Matter or wired (PoE) where you can.
2. **Skipping the UPS.** One power blip corrupts a NAS or SD card. A cheap
   battery backup pays for itself the first time.
3. **Running the OS off an SD card long-term.** Move Home Assistant / your
   homelab to SSD or NVMe. SD cards die.
4. **Wet 3D-printing filament.** Half of "my printer is broken" is actually
   moisture. A filament dryer fixes stringing and brittle prints.
5. **No off-box backup.** Self-hosting your photos is great until the drive dies.
   3-2-1 backup, always.

---

## The 10-minute setup order (do it in this sequence)

1. Hub online → 2. Radio plugged into a USB *extension* (not the hub directly —
avoids interference) → 3. Pair 2–3 sensors → 4. Write **one** automation you'll
actually use (e.g., "hallway light on at 5% after midnight when motion") →
5. Only then start adding more.

Getting one automation working beats buying twenty devices that sit unpaired.
