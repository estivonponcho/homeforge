# AI safety departures and warnings: a timeline without the hype

Researchers have raised different concerns at different times. A resignation, a public warning and an incident report are not interchangeable evidence.

**Published and reviewed: September 14, 2026.** Selected cases, not an exhaustive list. Dates below distinguish an announcement from later reporting where possible.

## March 22, 2023: an organized call for a pause

The [Future of Life Institute's open letter](https://futureoflife.org/open-letter/pause-giant-ai-experiments/) called for a pause of at least six months in training systems more powerful than GPT-4. This is a documented advocacy demand, not evidence that the industry implemented a pause. It is also different from a researcher's resignation.

## May 2023: Geoffrey Hinton leaves Google

Hinton left Google and spoke publicly about AI risks. The [University of Toronto's June 29 account](https://www.utoronto.ca/news/risks-artificial-intelligence-must-be-considered-technology-evolves-geoffrey-hinton) describes his wish to speak more freely and distinguishes existing harms from potential future risks. His departure should not be used as proof of a particular hidden incident at Google.

## May 2024: Jan Leike criticizes safety priorities at OpenAI

[AP's May 17 reporting](https://apnews.com/article/8a7ba341e06a66e9a7935bb06214edcb) describes Leike's resignation and his criticism that safety had lost priority to product development. This documents a departing researcher's assessment. It does not, by itself, establish every internal decision or predict a future catastrophe.

## February 9, 2026: Mrinank Sharma's resignation

[Forbes reported Sharma's departure](https://www.forbes.com/sites/conormurray/2026/02/09/anthropic-ai-safety-researcher-warns-of-world-in-peril-in-resignation/) and his broad warning about the state of the world. This is a separate episode from Coxon's September departure. A broad personal statement should not be rewritten as a disclosure of a specific technical failure without supporting evidence.

## September 2026: Jacob Coxon and the pacing debate

The recent departure and the subsequent public debate are covered in our [September explainer](ai-safety-frontier-debate-september-2026.md). Keep the original warning, the industry's responses, and evidence of implemented safeguards separate.

## What these cases have in common, and what they do not

**HomeForge analysis:** they raise recurring questions about who can challenge a development decision, who sees the evidence, and what happens when commercial goals conflict with caution. They do not establish a single coordinated story, a shared motive, or a reliable date for loss of control.

Avoid selecting only dramatic resignations and treating that sample as a poll of all researchers. Also avoid dismissing a substantive concern solely because its author resigned. Evaluate the specific argument, evidence and proposed remedy.

## How to evaluate the next story

The [deep research report](ai-safety-deep-research-departures-risks-mitigations.md) adds research-freedom disputes, the Right to Warn movement, company responses and a counterexample to inferred protest motives.

- Locate the original statement and the date, not just a screenshot of a headline.
- Separate what the person observed from what they predict.
- Identify whether the claim concerns privacy, discrimination, security, misinformation or loss of control.
- Look for the organization's response and independent evidence. Absence of a response in an article is not an admission.
- Ask what would confirm or disprove the claim, and what corrective action could be measured.
