# HomeForge content strategy

HomeForge should turn useful research into proof, and proof into a reader action:

> watch → build → document → recommend → email follow-up

The source research stays private. Public pages cite useful videos as curated references; they do not expose viewing dates, unrelated history, or describe the list as anyone's watch history.

## Editorial position

HomeForge is the practical, local-first layer between a good technical video and a finished weekend project. Each post should answer three questions:

1. What is worth building?
2. What did the build teach us that the video did not?
3. Which tools or parts genuinely made it easier?

## Four content clusters

| Cluster | Reader promise | First build-led article | Natural next step |
|---|---|---|---|
| Home Assistant + Zigbee | A smart home that keeps working without the cloud | A room-in-a-box: coordinator, contact sensor, presence sensor, and one useful automation | Starter Kit + smart-home picks |
| Tiny, efficient homelabs | Useful services without a rack or a power bill surprise | The $20–$100 always-on node: what belongs on it and what does not | Quadra build + homelab picks |
| Functional 3D printing | Print fixes, organizers, and upgrades—not shelf clutter | Five small prints that improve the HomeForge bench | Printer/filament picks |
| Private/local AI | Match model and hardware to a real job | A private HomeForge research assistant with Ollama + Open WebUI | Local-AI picks + newsletter |

## Twelve-week publishing rhythm

Repeat a three-part cycle for each cluster:

- Week A — **Resource:** a short curated watchlist with a clear learning goal.
- Week B — **Build:** the HomeForge implementation, including mistakes and photos.
- Week C — **Decision:** a comparison or buyer's guide based on the build.

One build therefore produces a site article, a newsletter, a short community post, and several product links without manufacturing four separate ideas. The weekly automation may draft from completed, documented work only; it must never invent test results.

## First project queue

1. **Room-in-a-box Home Assistant starter build** — Home Assistant Green or mini PC, Zigbee coordinator, one contact sensor, one presence sensor, and a lighting automation.
2. **Tiny-server power and usefulness audit** — compare the existing Quadra node with a mini PC by workload, noise, maintenance, and measured wall power.
3. **Print the homelab tidy-up kit** — cable labels, drive tags, mini-PC mount, sensor enclosure, and filament-safe storage labels.
4. **Private AI research desk** — Ollama + Open WebUI on existing hardware, with a small set of repeatable HomeForge research prompts.

## Quality and revenue rules

- Publish measurements only after recording them; label estimates as estimates.
- Prefer durable tutorials over model-release news. Use release news only when it changes a real HomeForge workflow.
- Each article gets one primary call to action: Starter Kit, newsletter, related build, or picks—not all four at equal weight.
- Add affiliate links only where the linked item is needed or meaningfully compared.
- Recheck external videos quarterly; replace removed or materially outdated references.
